## Answer

### Fundamental Equipment and Setup Requirements
Choosing equipment is a fundamental early decision in apiculture, balancing design trade-offs and standardized dimensions [SRC-7f9fb3402aa7].

* **Hive Designs**: The Langstroth hive is the dominant design globally, built around removable vertical frames spaced with a precise "bee space" (roughly 6-9 mm) [SRC-7f9fb3402aa7].
This spacing prevents bees from building comb across gaps or sealing them entirely with propolis, allowing individual frames to lift out cleanly [SRC-7f9fb3402aa7].
* **Langstroth Hive Stack Structure**: [SRC-7f9fb3402aa7]
* **Bottom Board**: Serves as the hive floor and features an entrance for the bees [SRC-7f9fb3402aa7].
* **Brood Boxes**: The lowest boxes where the queen lays eggs and the brood nest is maintained, typically reserved as the colony's unharvested space [SRC-7f9fb3402aa7].
* **Queen Excluder (Optional)**: A mesh grid featuring openings too small for the queen to pass through, keeping her restricted from honey supers so brood does not mix with harvestable honey [SRC-7f9fb3402aa7].
* **Honey Supers**: Boxes stacked above the brood nest designated for surplus honey harvest [SRC-7f9fb3402aa7].
* **Inner and Outer Covers**: Top layers providing insulation, ventilation, and weatherproofing [SRC-7f9fb3402aa7].
* **Frames and Foundation**: Each box contains standardized wooden or plastic frames holding foundation sheets embossed with hexagonal patterns for bees to draw out comb [SRC-7f9fb3402aa7].
Alternative designs, such as top-bar hives (including Kenyan and Tanzanian variants), utilize a different approach [SRC-7f9fb3402aa7].
