## Answer

In contemporary general English, man is most commonly defined as an adult male human being  (SRC-b50283393e34). [SRC-965b925aec0b]
Historically, the Old English word mann was gender‑neutral, meaning “person” or “human,” and the term man derived from Proto‑Germanic *mann‑ originally designated any human regardless of sex or age (SRC-965b925aec0b). [SRC-965b925aec0b]
Over time the sense “adult male” became dominant, a shift noted in the Wikipedia article. [SRC-965b925aec0b]

Etymologically, the word traces back to Proto‑Germanic *mann‑ and further to a Proto‑Indo‑European root *man‑/*mon‑/*men‑, with cognates in Sanskrit, Avestan, and Slavic languages (SRC-965b925aec0b). [SRC-965b925aec0b]
