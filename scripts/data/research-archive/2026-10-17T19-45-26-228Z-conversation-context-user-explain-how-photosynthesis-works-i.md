assistant: **How photosynthesis works (in simple terms)**  

Plants (and some algae and bacteria) capture sunlight with a green pigment called chlorophyll. Using that light energy, they combine water taken up from the roots with carbon dioxide from the air. The chemical reactions produce two things:

1. **Glucose (a sugar)** – this serves as food and energy for the plant.  
2. **Oxygen** – released into the atmosphere for us and other animals to breathe.

**Three key facts**

1. **Light‑dependent stage:** In the tiny “thylakoid” membranes inside leaf cells, chlorophyll absorbs light and splits water molecules, releasing oxygen and storing energy in carrier molecules (ATP and NADPH).  
2. **Calvin cycle (light‑independent stage):** Using the stored energy, the plant fixes carbon dioxide into glucose in the fluid‑filled “stroma” of the chloroplast.  
3. **Ecological impact:** The oxygen released fuels most aerobic life, and the glucose forms the base of food chains, supporting virtually all ecosystems.
