## Answer

**Astronomical Identity and Classification**
Polaris, commonly called the North Star, is a Cepheid variable star – a pulsating super‑giant whose brightness varies with a regular period. [SRC-17ec35e69771]
It is the nearest Cepheid to Earth, located about 445.5 light‑years away, and belongs to a triple‑star system. [SRC-17ec35e69771]
The bright component Polaris Aa is the Cepheid, while a fainter companion Polaris Ab orbits it with a period of 29.59 years[SRC-17ec35e69771].

**Historical Role in Navigation** [SRC-1e93812a3bb1]
Because Polaris lies nearly on the extension of Earth’s rotational axis, it appears almost stationary in the northern sky (unverified: no matching retrieved evidence).
Ancient mariners and travelers used this fixed point to gauge direction and estimate latitude, treating the star as a celestial compass (unverified: no matching retrieved evidence).
The blog notes that the North Star’s “steady position… inspired… navigation systems” and served as a “vital reference” for early navigators across many cultures[SRC-1e93812a3bb1].

**Cultural and Mythological Significance** [SRC-1e93812a3bb1]
Across civilizations the North Star has been associated with constancy, guidance, and divine order. [SRC-1e93812a3bb1]
The blog describes how societies such as the Egyptians, Chinese, and various Native American groups regarded it as a symbol of eternal stability, a “divine symbol… embodying eternal constancy,” and linked it to spiritual concepts like the axis of heaven or the universe’s central point[SRC-1e93812a3bb1].

**Modern Scientific Details** [SRC-1e93812a3bb1]
- **Distance:** ~445.5 ly (Gaia‑based measurement)[SRC-17ec35e69771]
- **Apparent magnitude:** not given in the sources, but the star is bright enough to be the most prominent northern star. [SRC-17ec35e69771]
- **Cepheid nature:** Period‑luminosity relation makes Polaris a key calibrator for cosmic distances[SRC-17ec35e69771].
- **Triple system:** Consists of Polaris Aa (the Cepheid), Polaris Ab (orbiting companion with a 29.59‑year period), and a more distant third component Polaris B[SRC-17ec35e69771].
- **Scientific relevance:** Its proximity allows detailed study of Cepheid pulsations, stellar evolution, and distance‑scale calibration. [SRC-17ec35e69771]
