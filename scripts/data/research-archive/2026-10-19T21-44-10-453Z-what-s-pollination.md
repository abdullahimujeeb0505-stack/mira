## Answer

### What is Pollination?

Pollination is the reproductive process in flowering plants where pollen is transferred from the male part of a flower (the stamen or anther) to the female part (the stigma) of a plant of the same species [SRC-3c350eef4295][SRC-76a16ba1409e].
This transfer enables fertilization, leading to seed development and plant reproduction. [SRC-718f063b9c06]

### How Pollination Works

1.
**Pollen Transfer**: Pollen moves from the male anther to the female stigma of the same or another flower [SRC-76a16ba1409e].
2.
**Pollen Tube Growth**: Once on the stigma, a pollen tube grows down through the style (a long stalk) toward the ovary [SRC-76a16ba1409e].
3.
**Fertilization**: Sperm cells travel down the tube into the ovule/ovary to fertilize the egg cells, resulting in a seed [SRC-76a16ba1409e].
4.
**Seed Dispersal**: The mature seed detaches from the parent plant and disperses to grow into a new plant [SRC-76a16ba1409e].

### Types and Agents

- **Self-Pollination**: Occurs when a plant is fertilized by its own pollen moving from its stamens into its ovule/stigma [SRC-3c350eef4295].
- **Cross-Pollination**: Occurs when pollen is transferred to a different plant of the same species [SRC-3c350eef4295][SRC-76a16ba1409e].
- **Pollination Agents**: Pollen movement is mediated by abiotic vectors (such as wind) or biotic vectors (such as bees and butterflies) [SRC-3c350eef4295][SRC-76a16ba1409e].
Plants attract animal pollinators using bright colors, pleasant scents, and energy-rich nectar [SRC-3c350eef4295].
