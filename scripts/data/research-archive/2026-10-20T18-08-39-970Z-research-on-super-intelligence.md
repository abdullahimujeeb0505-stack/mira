## Answer

**Definitions and theoretical frameworks**
Artificial superintelligence (ASI) is described as a hypothetical software‑based AI system whose intellectual capabilities exceed those of humans across all domains, possessing advanced cognitive functions beyond any human capacity [SRC-e4fa914d10f5].
The IBM overview situates ASI as a future state that would likely follow the achievement of artificial general intelligence (AGI), which can learn and reason broadly; the transition is theorized to involve recursive self‑improvement, a core element of many superintelligence models such as instrumental convergence and the intelligence‑explosion hypothesis (unverified: no matching retrieved evidence).

**Recent empirical and theoretical research** [SRC-896cb1601252]
The Francis Clase research portal lists several active studies that probe pathways toward superintelligence. [SRC-896cb1601252]
Notably, a 2025 “Capability Scaling Laws in Large Language Models” project is gathering data on how model size, compute, and emergent capabilities relate, explicitly testing whether capability emergence shows predictable scaling or discontinuous jumps that could signal threshold effects relevant to superintelligence theory [SRC-896cb1601252].
Another ongoing effort examines multi‑agent coordination to explore collective‑intelligence dynamics that might underpin distributed superintelligence scenarios [SRC-896cb1601252].
A completed 2024 systematic evaluation of reasoning capabilities across leading LLMs (GPT‑4, Claude, Gemini) establishes baseline performance and highlights current limitations, informing forecasts of when AGI‑level abilities may appear (unverified: no matching retrieved evidence).
