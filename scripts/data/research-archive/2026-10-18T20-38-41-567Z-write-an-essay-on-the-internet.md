Definition and Scope
The Internet is described as a global system of interconnected computer networks whose architecture has evolved organically from modest beginnings rather than a single grand design (unverified: no matching retrieved evidence).
This evolutionary nature is highlighted in the Internet Architecture Principles, which note that the Internet’s success stems from its continual change and adaptation [SRC-809384b4c841].

Origins and Governance
The early governance of the Internet was shaped by the Internet Architecture Board (IAB), originally formed as the Internet Configuration Control Board in 1979 by Vint Cerf at DARPA. [SRC-ba8e6e58c475]
It was later reorganized as the Internet Activities Board and finally became the IAB in January 1992, reflecting the transition of the Internet from a U.S. government project to an international public resource [SRC-ba8e6e58c475].
The IAB’s responsibilities include providing architectural oversight of Internet protocols, liaising with external organizations on behalf of the IETF, reviewing appeals of the standards process, and managing the RFC series and protocol parameter registries [SRC-ba8e6e58c475].
