## Answer

Pollination is the transfer of pollen from the male reproductive part of a flower (the anther, located on the stamen) to the female reproductive part (the stigma, which is part of the carpel/pistil) of the same or another flower, enabling fertilization and seed development [SRC-a876e11e4f1b][SRC-05057fe1c221].

A typical illustration of pollination shows a flower—often a lily—with its key structures labeled: the male side includes the anther and filament (together the stamen), while the female side displays the sticky stigma at the top of the style, the style itself, and the ovary at the base of the carpel. [SRC-05057fe1c221]
Pollen grains are depicted landing on the stigma, and a growing pollen tube is often shown extending down the style toward the ovary, illustrating the path of the male gamete toward the ovule. [SRC-a876e11e4f1b]
