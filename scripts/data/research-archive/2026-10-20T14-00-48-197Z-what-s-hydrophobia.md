## Answer

Hydrophobia is a noun meaning an extreme dread or fear of water. [SRC-b33bad08a0a7]
In modern usage it most often refers to the symptom seen in rabies infection, where painful throat spasms make swallowing liquids impossible, leading the patient to avoid water [SRC-c7a1f60c4467].
Historically, the word was also used as another name for rabies itself [SRC-c7a1f60c4467].

The term comes from Greek hydrophobía ( hydro ‘water’ + phobia ‘fear’), recorded in Late Latin and first attested in English around 1540‑1550 [SRC-c7a1f60c4467].

In medical contexts, hydrophobia describes the characteristic fear of water that results from involuntary throat muscle spasms in rabies patients. [SRC-c7a1f60c4467]
