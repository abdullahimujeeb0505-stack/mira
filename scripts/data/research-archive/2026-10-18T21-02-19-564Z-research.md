## Answer

Definition:
A question mark (?) is a punctuation mark used primarily at the end of a direct question, signaling to the reader that a query is being posed. [SRC-4fade99a6954]
It consists of a curved stroke with a dot beneath it. [SRC-4fade99a6954]

Standard Usage Rules in English: [SRC-4fade99a6954]
- **Direct questions**: End a sentence that asks for information with a question mark (e.g., “Where are you going?”). [SRC-aee047434268]
- **Tag questions**: Add a short interrogative tag to a statement, ending with a question mark (e.g., “You are coming, aren’t you?”). [SRC-aee047434268]
- **Rhetorical questions**: Use a question mark even when no answer is expected (e.g., “Who could have predicted that?”). [SRC-aee047434268]
- **Uncertain dates or facts**: Place a question mark after a date or fact that is doubtful (e.g., “Geoffrey Chaucer (1343?–1400)”). [SRC-aee047434268]
- **Indirect questions**: Do **not** use a question mark; treat them as statements and end with a period (e.g., “She asked whether the meeting was scheduled.”). [SRC-aee047434268]
- **Placement with quotation marks**: When a quoted sentence itself is a question, the question mark stays inside the closing quotation marks, following Chicago style conventions (unverified: no matching retrieved evidence).
- **Placement with parentheses**: If a whole sentence within parentheses is a question, the question mark goes inside the parentheses; if the surrounding sentence is a question, the mark goes outside.
