Provide a clear, concise definition of pollination, describe the basic mechanisms (biotic and abiotic), and explain its ecological and agricultural significance.

- What is pollination?

## Answer

Pollination is the transfer of pollen from the male part of a flower (the anther or stamen) to the female part (the stigma or carpel) of the same species, enabling fertilisation and seed production[SRC-a876e11e4f1b].
It occurs via biotic agents such as insects (bees, butterflies), birds and bats that actively carry pollen, and via abiotic agents like wind and water that disperse pollen passively[SRC-a876e11e4f1b].
After pollen lands on a compatible stigma, a pollen tube grows down the style to deliver male gametes to the ovule, where fertilisation forms a seed that develops into fruit[SRC-05057fe1c221].
Ecologically, pollination underpins the reproduction of most flowering plants, supporting biodiversity and providing food resources for humans, birds, mammals and other organisms[SRC-05057fe1c221].
Agriculturally, pollinator services—especially from honey bees and other insects—are essential for the production of over 100 U.S. crops, contributing to diverse and plentiful fruit, nut, and vegetable supplies[SRC-1e72a0f13d76].
